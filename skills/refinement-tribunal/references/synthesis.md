# Synthesis

Synthesis creates or destroys most of the value. Process each agent's output alone, in this order, before cross-referencing.

## Order

1. **Researcher** (skip if dropped). Mark each finding novel, already addressed, or not applicable. Novelty rate = novel / total. Rank novel findings by impact, weighting focus dimensions double.
2. **Advocate.** Build the protection list (standout strengths with HIGH or MEDIUM confidence) and the amplification list (high-impact elements with HIGH or MEDIUM confidence). Note effective patterns to reuse. Treat a STRENGTH EROSION flag as a regression signal. Compare this protection list with earlier iterations' lists to catch subtle erosion the advocate cannot see.
3. **Harsh critic.** Record the severity counts overall and per quality dimension. Flag any finding that targets a protected element. Draft changes for critical issues and significant gaps; for weaknesses where the researcher agrees; batch minor issues.

Reading the advocate before the critic keeps the synthesis out of a "fix everything" mindset that strips effective, unconventional choices.

## Cross-reference

| Agreement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| All three | Highest priority |
| Critic + researcher | Strong signal of a gap or flaw |
| Advocate + researcher | Strong signal of an effective pattern |
| Critic + advocate on one element | Strong and flawed at once: fix the flaw surgically, keep the strength |

## Conflicts

- **Critic versus a protected element.** A CRITICAL finding must be fixed while keeping the strength; if that is impossible, document why and accept the loss. Below CRITICAL, the critic carries the burden of proof: prefer the advocate unless the critic's evidence is specific and compelling.
- **Other conflicts.** Prefer the finding that cites concrete evidence, explains cause, and proposes a testable change. On a tie, trust the critic on flaws, the advocate on strengths, and the researcher on domain practice.
- **Never average.** Find the concern behind each position and look for a change that satisfies all of them. Only if none exists, take the better-evidenced side.

## Apply

Keep the current version as the rollback target, re-read the intent anchor, then apply in this order:

1. Findings all three agents agree on.
2. Critical issues not touching protected elements.
3. Resolved critic-advocate conflicts.
4. Significant gaps.
5. High-impact novel research findings.
6. Amplifications.
7. Weaknesses the researcher reinforces.
8. Effective patterns applied to new areas.
9. Remaining weaknesses.
10. Minor issues.

After each change, check that it does not undo an earlier fix, weaken a protected element, or regress any quality dimension. Make structural changes (reorder, merge, remove sections) when findings call for them; do not limit yourself to line edits. Label each change clear-cut or judgment call.

## Iteration record

Severity counts (overall and per dimension), researcher novelty rate or "dropped", strengths protected, amplifications applied, erosion flags, conflicts and resolutions, and changes applied versus findings discarded with reasons.
